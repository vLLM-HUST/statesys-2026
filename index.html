Why StateSys

State has become the critical systems resource.

Many services now depend on shared or long-lived state for performance, efficiency, and service quality. StateSys 2026 brings together work on how systems organize, access, schedule, evolve, migrate, and reuse state across cloud runtimes, data systems, storage stacks, serverless platforms, stream processing, graph systems, and model serving.

    1. 16:00– 16:20

      Energy-Efficient Resource Management for Scale-Up Database Systems using HARP

      Leonie Seelisch · Dresden University of Technology

      Abstract & presenter bio
      Abstract

      Database management systems are fundamental to modern computing and can be significant consumers of energy. At the same time, the emergence of heterogeneous processors, such as Intel's hybrid architecture combining high-performance P-cores with energy-efficient E-cores, creates new opportunities for reducing the energy cost of database workloads through resource-aware execution. HARP has demonstrated its ability to exploit such heterogeneous architectures by dynamically adapting resource allocations to application needs. In this work, we investigate whether these benefits can be extended to database systems by integrating HARP with DuckDB. Our approach improves the geometric mean energy efficiency over all scenarios by 1.19× at the cost of 0.86× performance, with peak energy savings of 2.21×, demonstrating the potential of application-aware resource management for energy-efficient database systems.

      Presenter biography

      Leonie Seelisch is a Ph.D. student at the Chair of Operating Systems at Dresden University of Technology. Her workshop paper builds on her master's thesis and explores energy-efficient databases using HARP, a resource management framework for heterogeneous desktop machines. Her doctoral research aims to apply the HARP approach to distributed systems.

    2. 16:20– 16:40

      SafeMem: Fault-Tolerant Memory Allocation Interface for Software-based Checksums

      Robin Thunig · TU Dresden, Germany

      Abstract
      Abstract

      Bit flips corrupt data in modern memories, and the prevailing hardware defence, SEC-DED ECC, gives no guarantees for multi-bit errors larger than two bits. Existing software defences add such redundancy automatically, yet rely on invasive compiler extensions. We present SafeMem, a fault-tolerant memory allocation interface that uses the new C++26 compile-time reflections to automatically protect dynamically allocated data. Differential in-memory checksums detect errors, and triplication with majority voting corrects them, including errors that SEC-DED ECC could not handle. In a preliminary evaluation on TACLeBench benchmarks, SafeMem reduces silent data corruptions by 95.0% (detection) and 87.7% (correction). C++26 reflections thus have the potential to offer a low-effort path to fault tolerance for C++ code.

    3. 16:40– 17:00

      Compressed Model-State Management for Heterogeneous LLM Fine-Tuning with FlagScale

      Yuchen Meng · Renmin University of China

      Abstract & presenter bio
      Abstract

      Low-rank adaptation reduces trainable parameters but leaves frozen weights and saved activations as substantial runtime state. MemRift reduces this state with lossless compression. Integrating that representation into a heterogeneous training stack, however, requires more than decoding tensors: the runtime must coordinate when state is materialized, consumed, and released without violating forward, backward, or device-execution semantics. We implement a framework-level compressed-state management path in FlagScale/Megatron that combines deferred weight materialization, autograd-aware activation recovery, asynchronous restoration, and consumer-aware buffer reclamation. The same model-level workflow runs across NVIDIA, Hygon, T-Head PPU, and MetaX accelerator backends. Across the evaluated platforms and models, stored BF16 weights are reduced by approximately one third, while the maximum executable training context length increases by 24.84–53.54% over matched LoRA baselines. Short-run loss traces provide an execution-level sanity check of the integrated training workflow. These results characterize the capacity benefits of compressed model-state management across heterogeneous accelerator backends.

      Presenter biography

      Yuchen Meng is a first-year Ph.D. student at Renmin University of China and an intern at the Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence (BAAI). Her research interests include large language model (LLM) systems, LLM post-training, memory-efficient fine-tuning, and heterogeneous computing. Her current work explores system-level techniques for improving the memory efficiency and scalability of LLM training across diverse accelerator platforms.

    4. 17:00– 17:20

      Online presentation

      Intrinsic Value Modules: Trusted-Hardware Support for Locally Final Offline Asset Transfer

      Ali Rahimi · Tabdeal Labs

      Abstract & presenter bio
      Abstract

      Consensus lets distributed participants agree on a common state, but it makes monetary validity depend on network connectivity and collective acceptance. We explore making validity intrinsic to a digital coin. We propose an Intrinsic Value Module (IVM): trusted hardware that gives a coin locally verifiable authenticity and spendability, analogous to verifying a signature. Under stated hardware and cryptographic assumptions, the IVM abstraction and our offline transfer protocol move monetary uniqueness from a replicated ledger into non-clonable protected state. The protocol prevents forgery, replay, double credit, and double spending by genuine, uncompromised IVMs; communication failures may lock value but cannot duplicate it. We distinguish local transfer atomicity and asset uniqueness, which the protocol establishes, from global state consistency across disconnected devices, which it does not. The IVM does not eliminate trust; it relocates trust to certified hardware, issuer roots, and non-rollback state. We present the architecture, protocol, and security analysis, and discuss open challenges.

      Presenter biography

      Ali Rahimi is a researcher at Tabdeal Labs, the research arm of Tabdeal, a leading digital-asset exchange in Iran. He holds an MS in Electrical Engineering and works on secure hardware and offline payment protocols for digital assets. His current research focuses on making monetary validity locally verifiable through trusted execution environments.

Topics

Topics of interest include the following areas.

State Access and Sharing

Access paths, shared state, locality, observability, concurrency, and cross-node access.

State Placement and Scheduling

Partitioning, placement, caching, tiering, and state-aware task and resource scheduling.

State Consistency and Coordination

Consistency models, synchronization, concurrency control, replication, and distributed coordination.

State Migration and Recovery

Migration, checkpointing, fault tolerance, elasticity, and online reconfiguration.

State Evolution and Reuse

Online updates, versioning, lifecycle management, and reuse across requests or tasks.

Stateful Systems and Applications

Data and storage systems, stream and graph processing, serverless platforms, and model serving.
